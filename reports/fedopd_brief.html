Operations research · not clinical · NHS RTT

Federated forecasting of NHS outpatient demand

Every NHS trust is a federated client that helps train one shared, per-department demand forecaster — without its raw data ever leaving the site. Those forecasts feed an agentic layer that turns them into staffing, allocation and capacity decisions.

FL plane — learns the forecast Agentic plane — acts on it
00

The problem, in one screen

England's hospitals report Referral-to-Treatment (RTT) waiting-time data every month. We forecast new demand for each trust × specialty, then decide where capacity should go. The signal is operational — counts of new referrals — never patient records.

What is learnable

A demand forecaster: a window of past months + calendar features → next month's new referrals. Swappable behind get_params / set_params (NumPy → PyTorch, without touching the FL loop).

Why not just pool the data

Trusts are governed separately and their case-mix differs — one is ophthalmology-heavy, another surgical. Raw records shouldn't leave a site, and one blunt global model under-serves the specialised trusts. That heterogeneity is exactly what makes federated learning + personalisation the right tool.

Two coupled planes over one hierarchy. The FL plane learns the forecast (global model flows down, model updates flow up). The agentic plane acts (allocations flow down, resource requests flow up). Keeping them separate is the point.

Step 3 — how we handle missing / empty data

The core of Step 3: an absent value can mean three different things, so we detect and treat each one differently rather than blanket-filling.

Structural zeros — 24%

A trust×dept that never appears = the trust doesn't run that specialty. Not imputed — it's legitimately absent. Handled by choosing a shared department set (Step 4) so no client is asked to forecast a service it doesn't provide.

True gaps — 416 series

A series that reports, stops, then resumes — an internal hole (4,267 month-cells total). Flagged now, filled in Step 5: short holes by seasonal / linear interpolation, long holes left masked (not fabricated).

Partial-history trusts — 34

A trust present in fewer than all 61 months (mostly 34–60). Held out of the day-1 balanced panel; revisited only if a region can't field 10 trusts.

Result: a balanced panel of 138 of 172 trusts present in all 61 months. Departments are ranked by ubiquity (share of balanced trusts offering them with full history) — 13 clear the 70% bar, so selecting 10 is comfortable.

Region attach — trust → ICB → region

Region isn't in the RTT files. We resolved it authoritatively from the NHS ODS ORD API: each ICB (Provider Parent) carries an RE2 / RO209 link to one of the 7 official NHS regions. All 42 ICBs resolved (0 unresolved); 172/172 trusts mapped. Every region has ≥10 balanced trusts — so the 7×10×10 panel is feasible everywhere.

03

How we select the federation

Target panel: 7 regions × 10 trusts × 10 departments ≈ 700 series × 61 months (floor of 5–6 trusts where a small region can't field 10). Selection is stratified & purposive, not random — random would hit broken histories and wouldn't guarantee the heterogeneity that justifies federated learning.

Trusts — filter, then stratify

Balanced panel

Present in all 61 months — forecasting can't tolerate gaps.

Shared departments

Offers every selected department, so FedAvg aligns the same feature set across clients.

Size mix

Deliberately span the ~100× demand spread — large teaching + medium + smaller trusts. This is what justifies per-client normalisation.

Departments — gate on two, rank on the third

CriterionRoleHow it's computed
UbiquitygateFraction of trusts reporting the dept in ≈all 61 months (months_present ≥ 57) → keeps the panel balanced.
VolumegateMedian monthly demand per trust (robust to a few huge trusts) → drops sparse, near-zero series.
Regional variationrankNormalise each region to a department-share vector, then measure how much a dept's share swings across the 7 regions (max−min share / coeff. of variation / contribution to Jensen–Shannon divergence).

Selection = gate then rank: keep departments passing ubiquity + volume, then take the top 10 by regional variation. Output is an auditable ranked table — you see exactly why each of the 10 is in. note there is no "Oncology" treatment function in RTT; we measure which specialties actually vary by region (e.g. Ophthalmology, Elderly Medicine) rather than assume.

04

End-to-end pipeline

From raw NHS files to agentic decisions. We're inside stage 3 now; the rest is scoped and locked.

1

NHS RTT data done

60 monthly "Full CSV" files scraped, downloaded, unzipped and verified (idempotent, stdlib-only). ~5 GB, git-ignored, re-fetchable.

2

Canonical table done

Keep Part_3 · trusts (R##) · drop C_999 · sum across commissioners → [date, branch, dept, demand].

3

EDA & client selection in progress

  • Missing-data rules → balanced-panel candidates
  • Attach region (trust → region lookup) → hierarchy counts
  • Ubiquity + volume + regional-variation → the 7×10×10 panel
4

Federated forecasting (cross-silo) next

Each trust trains locally; coordinator aggregates FedAvg → FedProx → personalised (shared backbone + per-trust head). Optional hierarchical trust→ICB/region→HQ. Raw data never leaves the trust.

5

Evaluate

MAE / sMAPE per trust and averaged, against baselines: each trust alone (lower bound) and a pooled model (upper bound). Personalisation is the expected winner on non-IID data.

6

Agentic decisions

Forecasts → capacity / staffing / service-line allocation. Allocate to trusts (directable, resourced); buy from independents (paid per activity).
